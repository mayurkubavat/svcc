Agent-assisted hardware design and verification workflows benefit from sub-second compile-and-run iteration in C++17 and IEEE 1666-2023 SystemC, yet tape-out sign-off requires IEEE 1800-2023 SystemVerilog RTL, IEEE 1800.2-2020 UVM, and SystemVerilog Assertions (SVA). Direct syntax translation or unverified language-model rewriting across these ecosystems silently corrupts semantics: garbage-collected UVM handles collide with C++ value-copy TLM ports, context-determined bitvector arithmetic diverges from C++ integer promotions, SystemVerilog Preponed #1step sampling races against SystemC delta notifications, and Boost.Proto expression templates in CRAVE exhibit superlinear C++ compile times on multi-clause constraints.

We present svcc, an open-source Rust compiler and translation-validation tool (12,305 lines of Rust across eight crates plus Python bindings) that translates bidirectionally between IEEE 1800-2023 SystemVerilog (RTL and UVM VIPs) and C++17 SystemC 3.0.3 (IEEE 1666.1 RTL, UVM-SystemC, CRAVE, FC4SC, and untimed C++17 golden models) via a seven-dialect IR (UVIR) with LCM dist scaling, per-age cover property counters, and 32-bit Feistel randc generation. Translations are checked without commercial simulators by four solver-backed engines: a Z3 QF_BV CRV/enum/dist miter; bounded model checking of C++17 SVA assertion and cover observers; UCIS 1.0 coverage isomorphism; and word-level Btor2 sequential equivalence checking across latency L >= 0. Across 20 designs, svcc translates files in 200 us median (sv2sc) and 210 us median (sc2sv), proves 20/20 round-trip and 8/8 C-to-RTL equivalences in 5.28-26.27 ms, catches 20/20 semantic bugs with 0/4 false alarms, recovers 12/12 standalone UVM files, cuts clang++ -O2 compile time on 120-clause CRAVE blocks by 5.95x, speeds up live CRAVE W=10 randc by 263x, and lowers 9/12 unmodified OpenTitan/PULP/OpenHW/Ibex files.
